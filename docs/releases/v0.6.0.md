# DSH for Android v0.6.0

Everything here came out of using the app on a phone, and two of the four bugs were
invisible to every test that existed when they were found.

## The homescreen widget

A card showing what needs you, which sessions are running **by name**, and when the app last
confirmed it. It costs nothing when the app is asleep: no WorkManager, no AlarmManager, no
boot receiver, `updatePeriodMillis` still 0. Nothing is written at all until a widget is
placed.

Its age is an absolute instant — `oldest reading 11 Oct 12:03` — rather than a ticking
counter. The date is always printed, because a bare `12:03` drawn yesterday reads as seven
minutes old just after noon. The instant cannot rot, needs no wake-up to withdraw it, and
survives force-stop and reboot by construction.

The headline is the most useful true fact rather than the same sentence every time:
`Working` when sessions are running and the host is talking, `Running` when they are listed
but it has gone quiet, `No agents active` only when the roster is known and genuinely empty,
and `Running: unknown` when it cannot be read at all.

**A battery bug it exposed.** `publishWidgetWorking()` ran on the app's one-second mux loop,
and the publisher gated only the *file write* on its heartbeat — `updateAppWidget` ran on
every publish. Up to 86,400 binder calls a day, screen on or off, widget on screen or not,
contradicting the class's own documentation. Draws are now gated on a changed value or the
15-minute heartbeat.

## Branching from an earlier conclusion

A branch icon in the assistant footer, beside Copy, on the row that closes any completed
turn — the web's own slot. Before it happens a dialog says what it does: a new session
holding the conversation to the end of that turn, opened straight away, with **this session
left alone**. The web does none of this; it forks on the tap.

## The sidebar

**Every workspace the registry reports is drawn, always** — including one whose only sessions
are archived, which used to vanish with no sign it existed. `No sessions`, `Sessions hidden
by filters` and `Sessions not loaded` are three different sentences for three different facts,
and the last prints no count at all rather than a zero it cannot vouch for.

A **collapsed** workspace holding live work now tints its header title blue, so a folded
folder cannot hide a running agent. The folder glyph keeps meaning "you are here" — one
accent token, never two meanings on one element. A group whose members have not all been
delivered is faded and says `Running: unknown` rather than being drawn as idle.

## The queue dock

Editing a queued message had been impossible for months, and the cause was not in the app:
the host moved the pending queue into the `inbox` session projection and stopped sending the
`queues`/`queue` frame the client read. The Edit control was always there, permanently
disabled, behind a condition that no longer held — and a local echo drew the same glyph with
every verb inert, so it looked like a feature that had never existed.

## Approvals

An accepted approval used to clear its own card **before anything was sent**, and the reply
was never inspected — a refused or lost answer rendered as accepted, with one `Log.d` line as
the only trace. A tap now moves the card to *Sending your answer…* with the pills genuinely
inert, an accepted answer leaves it as *Approval sent — waiting for the host to confirm*, and
only the host's own `cancel` removes it. A failure says so on screen, re-arms the pills, and
re-posts the notification. Nothing withdraws the notification while an answer is unconfirmed.

The question path is still optimistic and is recorded as open work.

## Smaller things

A clear-x on both search boxes, shown only when there is something to clear and withheld
from password, URL and key fields — a control offering to discard what you cannot read back
is a hazard, not a convenience. The widget's title cap no longer truncates at 32 characters
before the view truncates it again, which is what made a wide card look like it had large
margins, and its provider asks for 140dp of height rather than 90dp, which is why its third
row was clipped.

## A note on testing

732 tests across 76 suites. But four bugs this release fixed were found by a person looking
at a screen, not by the suite that existed when they shipped: a negative forty-six-year age,
a card with a centred column, an Edit that could never be enabled, and an approval that
cleared itself before asking. Each passed every test present at the time, because the tests
had been written from the same misunderstanding as the code.
